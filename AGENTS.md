# Agent instructions

See [README.md](README.md) for the content layout and how to build the site.

## Vision

The framework is for senior engineers and above who build API governance in their organizations. It is a starting point, not a complete reference: it skips basics its readers know, and picks one option where the industry offers several. It includes only approaches and tools that major companies adopt and document.

## Content

A pillar page stays conceptual: it names classes of tools but no formats, products, or vendors, and gives no references. It opens with a law that motivates the pillar, then says what the pillar aims for and what it relies on, linking its documents there and nowhere else. It then lists at least two concerns, followed by the actions that address them in the same order. Each action addresses exactly one concern; a concern may have several actions. A concern is one sentence that says what teams do or miss, how this plays out, and what it costs: delays for teams in the developer productivity pillars, disruption for customers in the customer satisfaction pillars. It keeps the reasoning that makes its actions necessary, and does not restate its name as its cause or cost. An action names one kind of document or one class of tools and the result it achieves, documents first. Fold a sentence that names neither into the action it qualifies.

A document page has two sections: intent and content. The intent says what the document defines and links the pillar that relies on it. The content treats the document as one whole with parts: guidance that applies to all parts, if any, then each part with its guidance followed by examples that highlight its key aspects.

A practice is named after the artifacts it introduces. A practice page has three sections: intent, approach, and examples. The intent says what to achieve and why, names its cost, then says how the practice supports each of its pillars, linking each. The approach lists steps in the order teams take them; each step achieves a result on its own, so teams can stop after any step.
